Decision help

How to choose

How much time can you commit?

Full-time bootcamps (General Assembly, Le Wagon) demand 9–12 weeks away from work. If you're employed and can only study part-time, shorter live courses (codencode.sg, NTUC LearningHub) or self-paced study fit better.

What can you fund or subsidise?

SCTP, IMDA TIPP and WSQ funding can cut costs by up to ~95% for eligible Singaporeans — but eligibility rules and course lists change, so always verify directly with the provider before assuming a subsidy applies.

Do you need a placement guarantee?

NUS-ISS SCTP is explicitly train-and-place. Most private bootcamps and short courses, codencode.sg included, focus on skills-building and interview readiness rather than a placement guarantee — set expectations accordingly.

Do you need teaching in your first language?

Most SG bootcamps teach English-only. If explanations in 中文 or Bahasa Melayu help concepts click faster for you, codencode.sg's trilingual format is a differentiator worth weighing against a larger brand name.

Where we fit

Where codencode.sg fits — honestly

We're not trying to be General Assembly or Le Wagon — those are larger, longer, full-time bootcamps with bigger brand recognition and (for GA in particular) established employer partnerships. codencode.sg is a smaller, trilingual, part-time-friendly option built for:

  • Working adults who can't take 9–12 weeks off for a full-time bootcamp
  • Learners who want explanations available in English, 中文 or Bahasa Melayu, not English-only
  • People who want a low-commitment way to test a course (free trial class) before paying
  • Beginners who want a focused, single-topic course (just Python, just cybersecurity) rather than a broad multi-month program

If you need a funded, placement-guaranteed career switch and qualify for it, NUS-ISS SCTP is worth checking first. If you want the most recognised bootcamp brand and can go full-time, General Assembly or Le Wagon are strong options. If you want live, flexible, trilingual teaching without quitting your job, that's where codencode.sg fits.

FAQ

Questions about choosing a provider

Free self-study (freeCodeCamp, official docs, YouTube) is the cheapest option at $0, but it requires strong self-discipline and offers no live feedback — most learners who struggle alone eventually pay for structured, live instruction instead.
General Assembly runs longer, higher-intensity full-time bootcamps aimed squarely at career switchers, while codencode.sg runs shorter, part-time, trilingual live courses better suited to working adults upskilling alongside a job.
Several providers including General Assembly, Le Wagon Singapore and NTUC LearningHub offer SkillsFuture Credit or SSG/WSQ-funded course options for eligible Singaporeans — always confirm current funding status directly with the provider, as eligible course lists change.
NUS-ISS's SkillsFuture Career Transition Programme (SCTP) is a strong option if you qualify for its train-and-place structure, while private bootcamps and shorter courses suit learners who want a faster, more flexible entry point without SCTP's eligibility requirements.
